/Cellular Automata (CA) model geometry by abstracting continuous space and its dynamics into a discrete framework of interacting cells. This allows for the simulation and study of complex spatial patterns, structures, and their evolution across various disciplines.

Here's how cellular automata fundamentally model geometry:

### 1. Discrete Spatial Representation

- **Grids and Lattices**: CA operate on a **regular grid (or lattice) of cells**, where space is discrete. These grids can be **one-, two-, or three-dimensional**, and even extend to _n_-dimensions.
- **Cell Shapes and Topologies**: While square grids are common, cells can also be arranged in other shapes like **hexagons or triangles**, especially in 2D, providing different symmetries and neighborhood properties. Irregular grids and even dynamically changing topologies have also been explored for more realistic modelling.
- **Discrete States**: Each cell occupies **one of a finite number of possible states** (e.g., 0 or 1, alive or dead). These states define the geometric content or property of that specific spatial point.

### 2. Local Interaction Rules for Dynamic Evolution

- **Local Rules**: The evolution of geometry in a CA is governed by a **fixed, local interaction rule** applied uniformly to all cells. This rule determines a cell's next state based on its current state and the states of its **local neighbours**.
- **Neighbourhood Definitions**: Different definitions of a cell's "neighbourhood" (e.g., von Neumann, Moore) influence how local interactions spread across the grid, thus affecting the resulting geometric patterns and their "shape".
- **Boundary Conditions**: To simulate finite or infinite spaces, CA employ specific boundary conditions, such as **periodic (toroidal) boundaries** (where edges wrap around to connect) or fixed boundaries, which significantly impact the overall geometric dynamics.

### 3. Emergent Complex Geometry

- **Self-Organization**: Despite simple local rules, CA can spontaneously generate **complex, self-organizing geometric patterns** and structures, which are not explicitly programmed into individual cells but emerge from their collective interactions.
- **Fractal Patterns**: Many CA rules generate **fractal patterns**, which exhibit self-similarity across different scales. A notable example is Rule 90, which produces a Sierpiński triangle.
- **"Computation as Representation"**: The very idea of CA uses **computation as a representation** for physical processes, where dynamics reduce to simple look-up tables based on neighbouring states, discretising everything.

### 4. Diverse Applications in Geometric Modeling

CA's ability to model geometry is evident in various fields:

- **Natural Pattern Formation**: CA model patterns seen in mollusc shells (e.g., _Conus textile_ resembles Wolfram's Rule 30), plant structures (stomata), and animal markings (zebra stripes, leopard spots).
- **Materials Science**: They simulate **crystal growth, solidification, and recrystallization phenomena**, tracking the evolution of microstructures, grain orientations, and boundaries.
- **Fluid Dynamics**: **Lattice Gas Cellular Automata (LGCA)** and **Lattice Boltzmann Models** use discrete particles on lattices (often hexagonal to ensure isotropy) to simulate fluid flows, recovering macroscopic equations like Navier-Stokes.
- **Urban Growth and Land Use**: CA simulate urban expansion, city forms, and spatial development by modelling local interactions between urban "cells," revealing self-organization and fractal characteristics in city growth.
- **Biological Morphogenesis**: CA are used in **Artificial Life** to model biological self-reproduction and morphogenesis, where local cellular interactions guide the development of complex organism shapes and patterns.
- **Computer Graphics and Art**: CA generate complex graphical effects, images, and animations, including maze generation and abstract art, by producing patterns with high aesthetic qualities and symmetries.
- **Fundamental Physics**: Some theories propose that the **universe itself might be a cellular automaton**, suggesting that physical laws and macroscopic geometry emerge from simple, local computational rules at a fundamental level.
- **Abstract Geometries**: CA have been studied on non-Euclidean spaces, such as hyperbolic geometries, demonstrating their versatility beyond simple Cartesian grids.

### 5. Advanced Geometric Modelling Techniques

- **Continuous Cellular Automata**: Variants like **SmoothLife** and **Lenia** use **real numbers for cell states** and smooth convolution kernels (instead of discrete counts) to model more "life-like" emergent patterns, effectively allowing for continuous state spaces and smoother geometric transitions.
- **Neural Cellular Automata (NCA)**: NCAs replace fixed rules with neural networks, allowing cells to learn geometric rules via gradient descent to produce predefined global patterns, demonstrating self-organizing and regenerative capabilities for tasks like **morphogenesis and image classification**.

In essence, cellular automata model geometry by constructing a **bottom-up simulation of space**. Instead of explicitly describing shapes and continuous transformations, they define local rules for discrete cells. These rules, when iteratively applied, lead to the **emergence of complex global patterns and structures** that mimic geometric forms and dynamics observed in the real world, from the microscopic scales of crystal lattices to the macroscopic forms of cities and biological organisms.
